# Stage 6 Parent-Theorem / Model Absorption Map

## Canonical mathematical object

The most important application-neutral object is the source duopoly reduced sourcing game:

- symmetric players;
- compact continuous actions \(x,y\in[0,\phi]\);
- action lowers own downstream marginal cost linearly;
- downstream homogeneous-product Cournot quantities are nonnegative;
- reduced upstream payoff is continuous, piecewise quadratic and globally strictly concave in own action under the source SOC;
- the downstream active set changes with upstream actions;
- the global BR may have negative, zero, and positive slope pieces;
- one parameter region has a symmetric equilibrium plus an asymmetric pair;
- one knife edge has a continuum.

## Absorption records

### A. Standard linear Cournot KKT / concave potential

Mapping:
- sourcing profile \(\mapsto\) downstream cost vector;
- nonnegative quantity equilibrium \(\mapsto\) KKT maximizer of a strictly concave potential.

Result:
- C1 is standard and directly absorbed.
- This parent theory does not solve the induced Stage-I sourcing game.

Verdict: **DIRECTLY ABSORBED for C1 only**.

### B. König (2010)

Mapping:
- continuum of inputs;
- outsourcing lowers marginal costs in the relevant alternative specification;
- Cournot competition;
- outsourcing share responds to number of firms.

Result:
- negative competition effect is already known in a close outsourcing specification.
- König treats Shy–Stenbacka's positive result as a contrast and does not derive the negative sign from their equation (13).

Verdict: **PARTIALLY ABSORBED**.

### C. Amir (2000)

Mapping:
- first-stage cost-reducing R&D;
- second-stage symmetric linear Cournot;
- endogenous firm asymmetry / possible exit.

Mismatch:
- polar R&D outcomes depend on the R&D return structure;
- not the Shy–Stenbacka continuous sourcing box with globally concave own reduced payoff.

Verdict: **GENERIC ASYMMETRY/EXIT ABSORBED; C7/C8 NOT DIRECTLY ABSORBED**.

### D. Amir, Garcia & Knauff (2010)

The general symmetry-breaking classes require nonconcavity together with strategic substitutability and produce only asymmetric pure equilibria in the main class.

Shy–Stenbacka correction:
- own reduced payoff globally strictly concave under source SOC;
- symmetric equilibrium survives;
- asymmetric pair can coexist with it;
- positive-slope piece comes from downstream exit.

A mapping would violate the hypotheses that generate their headline theorem.

Verdict: **NOT ABSORBED**.

### E. Amir, Halmenschlager & Jin (2011)

Mapping:
- two-stage R&D + Cournot;
- ex ante identical firms;
- asymmetric R&D / endogenous exit.

Mismatch:
- paper deliberately uses mildly rather than strongly decreasing R&D returns to generate polarization;
- source correction works under strong global own concavity.

Verdict: **GENERIC POLARIZATION ABSORBED; SOURCE RESULT NOT DIRECTLY ABSORBED**.

### F. Buehler & Schmutzler (2008)

Mapping:
- cost-reducing investment;
- downstream Cournot;
- asymmetric equilibrium.

Mismatch:
- asymmetry is generated by an endogenous discrete vertical-integration structure;
- without the integration asymmetry, their firms choose identical investment levels.

Verdict: **PARTIALLY ABSORBED**.

### G. Eckert, Klumpp & Su (2017)

Mapping:
- first-stage competitiveness investment;
- second-stage Cournot;
- multiple asymmetric equilibria.

Full-text check:
- baseline investment is binary/lumpy;
- downstream quantities are assumed positive for every baseline cost combination;
- extensions add more discrete investment choices.

Mismatch:
- source correction has a continuous sourcing action;
- downstream exit / active-set switching is itself the source of global BR geometry.

Verdict: **GENERIC MULTIPLE INVESTMENT EQUILIBRIA ABSORBED; SOURCE ACTIVE-SET THEOREM NOT**.

### H. Lamantia, Pezzino & Tramontana (2018)

Mapping:
- innovation and Cournot;
- discontinuous/piecewise best responses;
- symmetric and asymmetric multiple equilibria.

Mismatch:
- fixed-cost innovation architecture;
- different action and discontinuity mechanism.

Verdict: **GENERIC PIECEWISE-BR MULTIPLICITY ABSORBED; SOURCE CORRECTION NOT**.

### I. Spatial-price parent class

Vogel (2008) and related work make heterogeneous marginal costs and equilibrium refinements standard in spatial competition.

Gill & Thanassoulis (2016) show that \(p\ge MC\) is a recognizable Hotelling-line modeling restriction.

Verdict:
- generic H1/H3 narrative not novel;
- exact sourcing-induced source defect / H5-NL threshold not directly absorbed.

## Stage-6 theorem novelty conclusion

No broad “new game-theory mechanism” claim survives.

The surviving unit is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{new source-specific result/correction within a known model}
}
\]

rather than:

\[
\boxed{
\text{new general theorem about investment games}.
}
\]
