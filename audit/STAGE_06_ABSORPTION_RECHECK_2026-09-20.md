## 2. Parent-theorem attacks repeated

### Amir, Garcia & Knauff (2010)

Their symmetry-breaking synthesis identifies classes with only asymmetric pure equilibria using strategic-substitute structure together with payoff nonconcavity. The repaired source game differs materially:

- own reduced sourcing payoff is globally strictly concave;
- a symmetric equilibrium survives;
- the asymmetric pair coexists with that symmetric equilibrium;
- cap geometry is essential to the all-active example.

The manuscript's C8 theorem is therefore not obtained as a direct specialization of their headline symmetry-breaking class.

### Amir (2000); Amir, Halmenschlager & Jin (2011)

These papers absorb broad economic narratives of cost-reducing investment, polarization, asymmetry, and possible endogenous exit. They remain prior art for those generic mechanisms. The manuscript makes no generic novelty claim on those dimensions.

### Buehler & Schmutzler (2008)

Endogenous vertical integration can create asymmetric investment incentives and asymmetric organizational outcomes. This likewise absorbs broad asymmetry narratives but does not directly deliver the source model's cap-aware pure equilibrium correspondence.

### König (2010)

The fresh source recheck confirms that a negative competition effect on outsourcing is already present in König's **reversed / marginal-cost-saving outsourcing motive**. This continues to kill any generic novelty claim that competition can reduce outsourcing. The surviving result is the source-equation correction in Shy--Stenbacka.
