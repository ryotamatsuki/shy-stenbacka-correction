## 1. Executive novelty verdict

The correction project survives Stage 2, but its contribution must be stated more narrowly than “competition can reduce outsourcing.”

### Canonical verdict

[
oxed{	extbf{GO — GO TO MECHANISM SEARCH}}
]

The literature search found a serious prior-art threat: **König (2010), “Outsourcing motives, competitiveness and taxation.”** König explicitly adapts the Shy–Stenbacka (2005) framework and, in an extension with the same broad outsourcing motive of lower marginal cost but an outsourcing fixed-cost disadvantage, derives that more Cournot competitors can reduce outsourcing.

This means the **generic economic sign** “more Cournot competition can reduce outsourcing” is not novel.

However, König does **not** identify equation (14) of Shy–Stenbacka (2005) as algebraically wrong. He explicitly treats Shy–Stenbacka's positive result as a valid opposite result and attributes the difference to the cost structure. The searches conducted here likewise did not locate a paper that:

1. corrects equation (14) within the published Shy–Stenbacka source model;
2. identifies the missing hard outsourcing bound (0le i_jlephi);
3. identifies the all-active Cournot continuation failure at feasible asymmetric Stage-I histories; or
4. identifies the Hotelling interior-share continuation failure and constructs a profitable corner-regime deviation against the reported symmetric candidate.

Accordingly, the project survives as a **source-specific correction and global equilibrium re-characterization in a known model**, not as a new outsourcing mechanism.

Search failure is not proof of novelty. The classifications below are therefore `POTENTIALLY NOVEL`, not a claim that no prior disclosure exists anywhere.

## 3. Literature-family map

### Family A — Source lineage and strategic outsourcing foundations

- Shy and Stenbacka (2003), *Strategic Outsourcing*.
- Shy and Stenbacka (2003 draft), *Partial Subcontracting, Monitoring Cost, and Market Structure*.
- Shy and Stenbacka (2005), final Canadian Journal of Economics article.

This family establishes the source architecture and already contains strategic-outsourcing and strategic-substitute themes. These are not novel contributions of the correction.

### Family B — Competition intensity and outsourcing motives

**König (2010)** is the closest threat.

He uses a continuum-of-inputs Cournot framework explicitly adapted from Shy–Stenbacka. In the main fixed-cost-saving motive, more competition raises outsourcing. In an extension where outsourcing instead has the marginal-cost advantage—the broad motive relevant to Shy–Stenbacka—more firms reduce outsourcing.

König nevertheless writes that Shy–Stenbacka obtain the opposite result and attributes the difference to increasing versus constant marginal costs. Thus he does not expose the algebraic sign error in their equation (14).

### Family C — Partial outsourcing under uncertainty / real options

- Alvarez and Stenbacka (2007), *Partial outsourcing: A real options perspective*.
- Gao and Driouchi (2018), ambiguity/trust real-options extension.

These papers study optimal partial outsourcing under uncertainty. Their strategic architecture is not the two-stage oligopoly continuation problem at issue here.

### Family D — Make-and-buy / bargaining / nested sourcing

- Shy and Stenbacka (2012), nested versus horizontal outsourcing.
- Stenbacka and Tombak (2012), bargaining-power mechanism.
- Gehrig and Stenbacka (2023), dual sourcing and resilience.

These share sourcing and monitoring/bisourcing themes but change the players, objectives, bargaining or dynamic structure.

### Family E — Strategic partial outsourcing with supplier market power

- Chen and Chen (2020), *Strategic partial outsourcing in the presence of single-source components*.

Partial outsourcing is driven by supplier market power and capacity/sourcing structure, not the Shy–Stenbacka oligopoly game.

### Family F — Procurement / information design

- Rosar (2017), *Strategic outsourcing and optimal procurement*.

Outsourcing changes procurement/intermediation and private-information mechanisms. The game is structurally different.

### Family G — Discrete organizational choice under product-market competition

- Hamada (2010), *Outsourcing versus In-house Production: The Case of Product Differentiation with Cost Uncertainty*.
- Miyamoto (2021), strategic outsourcing and quality choice.

Hamada is particularly informative because it explicitly repeats Shy–Stenbacka's positive-competition claim as an existing result, then studies a discrete outsourcing/in-house choice under asymmetric information. This is evidence that at least some downstream literature propagated rather than corrected the disputed source conclusion.

## 6. Whole-game absorption test

### Question 1: Is there one prior model that reproduces the correction target by relabeling or restriction?

**No located prior paper does so.**

The underlying economic game itself is of course not new—it is Shy–Stenbacka (2005). The novelty unit is the **correction/re-characterization** of that exact game.

König (2010) comes closest, but the objective/cost structure differs. König assumes constant marginal costs in the adapted model and explicitly identifies that difference from Shy–Stenbacka's increasing input-cost structure.

Hamada (2010) changes the organizational action set from a continuous partial-outsourcing decision to a discrete outsourcing/in-house choice and adds asymmetric information.

Other successors change market structure, bargaining, supplier market power, procurement, uncertainty, or dynamics.

### Question 2: Is the source-specific sign correction an immediate corollary of a known theorem?

No general theorem located in this Stage-2 search was shown to specialize directly to the exact Shy–Stenbacka equation-(13) derivative.

The derivative correction is elementary once equation (13) is written correctly. Therefore the contribution is not mathematical sophistication; it is identification of a published error with downstream economic consequences.

### Question 3: Does König (2010) absorb the corrected negative sign?

**Partially, economically; not source-specifically.**

König establishes a negative competition effect in a close variant. Thus the economic statement “more competitors may reduce outsourcing” is known.

But König explicitly treats the Shy–Stenbacka positive result as different, rather than deriving the negative sign from their own equation. Therefore he does not absorb the claim:

> “Within the published Shy–Stenbacka (2005) source model, equation (14) has the wrong sign and Proposition 3 reverses.”

## 12. Strongest prior-art threat

### Jan König (2010), *Outsourcing motives, competitiveness and taxation*

This is the paper that a referee is most likely to use against an overbroad novelty claim.

Why it is dangerous:

- explicitly adapts Shy–Stenbacka (2005);
- uses a continuum of inputs and Cournot oligopoly;
- studies outsourcing proportion as a function of the number of competitors;
- in a marginal-cost-saving outsourcing extension, obtains less outsourcing when (N) increases;
- retains strategic-substitute outsourcing decisions.

Why it does **not** kill the correction:

- its marginal-cost structure differs;
- it does not derive its negative sign from Shy–Stenbacka equation (13);
- it explicitly says Shy–Stenbacka obtain the opposite result and explains the contrast as a model difference;
- it does not identify the source strategy-bound, Cournot active-set, or Hotelling continuation defects.

### Required positioning consequence

The eventual introduction should acknowledge König early and say, in substance:

> A negative competition–outsourcing relation has appeared in a related specification. The present contribution is different: it shows that the published Shy–Stenbacka formula itself implies the opposite sign from that reported, and that additional feasibility and continuation issues alter the equilibrium characterization of the original model.

Do not write “we are the first to show that competition can reduce outsourcing.”

**Risk:** too narrow; König (2010) substantially weakens the economic novelty.

### Route B — Constrained Cournot correction

Sign reversal + (ilephi) + all Cournot active sets + complete constrained equilibrium correspondence.

**Potential:** materially stronger, source-specific correction.

### Route C — Full Cournot + Hotelling global correction

Route B plus complete Hotelling price-subgame continuation and exact global outsourcing equilibrium/survival regions.

**Potential:** strongest surviving route. It addresses both the headline sign error and a distinct SPNE/globality failure.

Stage 3 should attempt to kill Routes A/B/C against tractability and contribution discipline, with a presumption in favor of the **minimal architecture that yields a complete publishable correction**, not a larger model.
