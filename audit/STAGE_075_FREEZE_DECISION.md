## 2. Mechanism card

### Phenomenon

A firm can lower its downstream marginal cost by increasing outsourcing / cost-reducing organizational investment.

### Friction

Outsourcing carries convex monitoring cost, and downstream quantities are constrained to be nonnegative. Hence sufficiently aggressive cost reduction can push a rival from positive output to zero output.

### Strategic response

Inside the all-active Cournot region, the source's local strategic-substitutes logic operates.

At the rival-exit boundary, however, the relevant global best-response geometry changes. A firm may need **more** own outsourcing when the rival outsources more in order to maintain the rival-exit outcome.

### Equilibrium effect

The complete best response is piecewise. It can contain:

- a decreasing all-active branch;
- a positive-slope rival-exit branch;
- flat/cap branches.

This overturns the source's global monotonicity claim and, in the source duopoly, can generate:

- a unique symmetric equilibrium;
- a continuum at a knife edge;
- or one symmetric equilibrium coexisting with an asymmetric pair.

### Welfare effect

The equilibrium multiplicity is welfare-material. Different certified Cournot equilibria can generate different total welfare.

This does not imply a policy prescription because the model has no policy instrument and welfare rankings are selection-dependent.

### Empirical implication

Empirical sourcing responses should differ between:

- markets in which all downstream firms remain active; and
- markets close to zero-output / exit thresholds.

The model predicts possible sign changes and sourcing asymmetry near the latter regime.

## 3. Can the mechanism be explained without notation?

Yes.

> The original analysis differentiates an interior equilibrium as though all firms always remain active. But outsourcing changes marginal cost. Once one firm's outsourcing is large enough to drive a rival's Cournot output to zero, the continuation game changes regime. That regime switch changes the slope of the sourcing best response and can create asymmetric equilibria even though each firm's sourcing payoff remains globally concave in its own action. The published local comparative statics therefore do not characterize the global game.

This is economically intelligible without equation-level detail.

The Hotelling section has a related but distinct lesson:

> once outsourcing-induced cost differences are large enough to move the price game to a corner, the interior pricing formulas no longer define a unique literal continuation.

## 5. Essential assumptions versus tractability assumptions

### Essential for the primary global Cournot mechanism

1. The upstream choice lowers own downstream marginal cost.
2. Downstream output cannot be negative.
3. Upstream choices can be large enough that the downstream active set changes.
4. The upstream action set contains the relevant exit-inducing region.
5. Firms choose sourcing before downstream competition.

Without an endogenous downstream participation boundary, the positive-slope rival-exit segment disappears.

### Essential for the exact source theorems

1. Linear inverse demand.
2. Linear marginal-cost reduction \(c_i=C_0-Hi_i\).
3. Quadratic monitoring cost \(i_i^2\).
4. Box constraint \(i_i\in[0,\phi]\).
5. The source SOC.
6. Pure-strategy Stage-I equilibrium scope for the complete duopoly correspondence.

These assumptions generate the exact thresholds and formulas.

### Tractability / normalization devices

1. \(S=0,\alpha=1\) under the source Assumption 2 normalization.
2. The dimensionless duopoly variables
   \[
   \delta=D/H,\qquad \rho=b/H^2.
   \]
3. Symmetric notation for stating the corrected general-\(N\) action.
4. Relabeling the source model as a cost-reducing organizational-investment game.

These do not create the mathematical defect being corrected.

### Hotelling-specific assumptions

- linear transportation cost;
- full coverage;
- fixed endpoint locations;
- pure pricing;
- the explicit \(p\ge c\) domain for the auxiliary no-loss result.

The last item is not a source assumption and must remain conditional.

## 9. Closest-paper distinction

The closest prior literature already contains each generic ingredient separately: König (2010) shows that competition can reduce outsourcing in a related specification; Amir and coauthors show that symmetric cost-reducing-investment games can generate asymmetry and exit; later Cournot investment papers generate multiple asymmetric equilibria; spatial-price papers study heterogeneous costs and price-domain restrictions.

The surviving distinction is therefore not that outsourcing can fall with competition, that investment can cause exit, or that asymmetric equilibria can exist. The contribution is that **the exact published Shy–Stenbacka model itself was globally mischaracterized**. Under the paper's own SOC, each firm's reduced sourcing payoff is globally strictly concave, yet respecting nonnegative downstream output introduces an active-set kink that invalidates the paper's global strategic-substitutes claim and changes the pure equilibrium correspondence. The paper also contains an independent sign error in its central Cournot comparative static. No located parent theorem or prior correction directly supplies this exact package.

This is sufficiently precise for a correction paper and sufficiently narrow to avoid priority overclaiming.

## 10. Welfare / organizational relevance

The welfare case is meaningful but secondary.

The corrected multiple equilibria are not merely relabelings: exact Stage-7 regressions show different certified equilibria can have different total welfare.

Therefore the equilibrium correction can matter for allocation, not just notation.

However:

- there is no selection-free global welfare ranking;
- the full planner problem is nonconvex because of the source monitoring technology;
- the model contains no policy instrument.

Hence welfare supports the importance of obtaining the equilibrium set correctly, but does not justify expanding the paper into a policy article.

The organizational implication is stronger and cleaner:

> organizational sourcing choices can alter downstream market participation, so an interior-only outsourcing analysis can misstate both strategic interaction and equilibrium multiplicity.

## 11. Would a skeptical field referee see more than a parameter exercise?

Yes, provided the manuscript is disciplined.

The strongest defense is structural:

1. the source's regular formula is only an all-active continuation;
2. the primitive strategy domain permits histories outside that region;
3. the globally valid continuation is analytically solvable;
4. the resulting Stage-I payoff remains globally own-concave;
5. nevertheless the cross-strategic geometry changes at endogenous participation thresholds;
6. this invalidates a published proposition and changes the equilibrium set.

That is more than changing a parameter value or choosing a numerical counterexample.

What would make the paper look like a parameter exercise:

- presenting only the \(\rho=3/5\) witness;
- claiming novelty from the \(+2\) slope alone;
- overemphasizing the Hotelling no-loss auxiliary model;
- adding welfare computations without a clear correction purpose.

Stage 10 must avoid those presentation failures.
