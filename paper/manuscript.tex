\begin{abstract}
We revisit the outsourcing model of Shy and Stenbacka (2005) using the published sourcing bounds, nonnegative Cournot quantities, and global product-market continuations.  In the Cournot model, the competition comparative static reported in equation (14) and Proposition 3 has the wrong sign: the corrected symmetric pure sourcing action is weakly decreasing across admissible integer market sizes and strictly decreasing off the sourcing cap.  Global continuation analysis also shows that Proposition 5 is only a regular-branch strategic-substitutes result.  In the source duopoly, the pure global best response can contain a positive-slope rival-exit segment, yielding a complete pure Stage-I correspondence with a symmetric equilibrium, an asymmetric pair, or a knife-edge continuum depending on parameters.  In the literal Hotelling game, sufficiently asymmetric sourcing histories generate multiple pure corner price continuations.  Under a separate no-loss restriction \(p_j\ge c_j\), the pure continuation is unique and the published symmetric sourcing candidate fails on an exact nonempty parameter region.  The results are source-specific corrections, not generic outsourcing theorems.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} outsourcing; strategic sourcing; Cournot competition; equilibrium correspondence; Hotelling competition
