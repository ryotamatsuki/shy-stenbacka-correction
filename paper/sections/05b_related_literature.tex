\section{Relation to the literature}
\label{sec:literature}

The contribution here is deliberately source-specific.  We correct and globally recharacterize the model of \cite{ShyStenbacka2005}; it does not claim a new general theorem that competition reduces outsourcing, that investment games generate asymmetric equilibria, or that no-loss pricing is novel.

Subsequent outsourcing research studies a variety of mechanisms, including real options \cite{AlvarezStenbacka2007}, organizational forms \cite{ShyStenbacka2012,StenbackaTombak2012}, procurement and strategic sourcing \cite{Rosar2017}, component structure \cite{ChenChen2020}, and quality choice \cite{Miyamoto2021}.  Most importantly for the competition comparative static, \cite{Konig2010} already obtains a negative competition--outsourcing relationship in a related outsourcing specification and explicitly treats the positive result in \cite{ShyStenbacka2005} as a contrast.  Our result is narrower: equation (14) in the published Shy--Stenbacka model itself has the opposite sign from the one reported there.

The asymmetric-equilibrium result also belongs to a well-developed strategic-investment literature.  Cost-reducing investment followed by Cournot competition can generate asymmetry, endogenous exit, or polarization \cite{Amir2000,AmirHalmenschlagerJin2011}; broader work studies symmetry breaking \cite{AmirGarciaKnauff2010}, investment asymmetry in vertically related settings \cite{BuehlerSchmutzler2008}, multiple asymmetric investment equilibria \cite{EckertKlumppSu2017}, and discontinuous or piecewise innovation responses \cite{LamantiaPezzinoTramontana2018}.  What is specific here is the correction inside the published sourcing model: own reduced payoff remains globally strictly concave under the source condition, while the nonnegative-output constraint creates an active-set kink that changes the global sourcing best response and the pure equilibrium correspondence.

The Hotelling result is likewise not a claim of a new general spatial-pricing phenomenon.  Heterogeneous-cost spatial competition is standard \cite{Vogel2008}, and explicit no-loss price restrictions appear in related pricing models \cite{GillThanassoulis2016}.  Our literal-source result is instead that sufficiently asymmetric sourcing histories move the published Hotelling continuation outside its interior branch and generate multiple pure corner continuations.  The \(p_j\ge c_j\) analysis is a separate robustness exercise used to show that the published symmetric sourcing candidate can also fail under a transparent no-loss strategy restriction.

These distinctions matter for interpretation.  The results establish a correction and a complete pure-strategy re-characterization of a specific published model.  They should not be read as establishing generic comparative statics for arbitrary demand, monitoring technologies, or mixed-strategy equilibrium sets.
