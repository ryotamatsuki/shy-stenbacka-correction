\section{Global Cournot continuation and best responses}
\label{sec:bestresponses}

The central issue is globality.  The all-active Cournot formula is valid only while every implied quantity is nonnegative.  Once a sourcing deviation changes the downstream active set, the reduced Stage-I payoff changes branch.

\subsection{The Stage-II continuation}

For a fixed sourcing profile, let \(c_{(1)}\le\cdots\le c_{(N)}\) denote the ordered marginal costs.  For any candidate active set \(S\), define
\begin{equation}
p_S=\frac{a+\sum_{j\in S}c_j}{|S|+1}.
\label{eq:pS}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Global Cournot continuation]
\label{lem:cournot-continuation}
For every feasible sourcing history, the Stage-II Cournot game has a unique pure quantity equilibrium.  Its active set \(S\) satisfies
\[
c_j<p_S\quad(j\in S),
\qquad
c_j\ge p_S\quad(j\notin S),
\]
and
\begin{equation}
q_j=
\begin{cases}
(p_S-c_j)/b,&j\in S,\\
0,&j\notin S.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:active-set-q}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

The result can be seen from the strictly concave exact potential
\begin{equation}
\Phi(q)=
\sum_j(a-c_j)q_j
-\frac b2Q^2
-\frac b2\sum_jq_j^2,
\label{eq:potential}
\end{equation}
whose Hessian is \(-b(I+\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top)\).  Thus the continuation is well defined even after sourcing deviations that invalidate the published all-active expression.

\subsection{Global concavity in own sourcing}

Fix rivals' sourcing choices and vary \(x=i_j\).  On a branch with \(m\) active firms including \(j\), let \(z_j=p-c_j>0\).  Then
\[
\frac{dz_j}{dx}=H\frac{m}{m+1}
\]
and
\begin{equation}
\Pi_j''(x)=
2\frac{H^2m^2}{b(m+1)^2}-2<0
\label{eq:branch-curvature}
\end{equation}
under \eqref{eq:source-soc}.  At own entry, \(z_j=0\), so the first derivative joins continuously.  At a rival-exit threshold, the marginal operating benefit drops because \(m/(m+1)>(m-1)/m\).  Hence the derivative has a downward jump.

\begin{lemma}[Global own-payoff concavity]
\label{lem:global-concavity}
Under \eqref{eq:source-soc}, each firm's reduced Cournot payoff is globally strictly concave in its own sourcing action on \([0,\phi]\), for every fixed rivals' sourcing profile.
\end{lemma}

Global concavity makes the constrained first-order/KKT condition sufficient for a best response, but it does not imply uniqueness of the complete Stage-I game.

\subsection{Corrected symmetric action and competition comparative static}

At a symmetric profile \(i_j=s\), all firms are active and
\[
q(s)=\frac{D+Hs}{b(N+1)}.
\]
The unconstrained stationary root is
\begin{equation}
\bar i_C
=
\frac{HND}{b(N+1)^2-H^2N}.
\label{eq:iCbar}
\end{equation}
The denominator is strictly positive under \eqref{eq:source-soc}.  The first-order condition here is the derivative of a unilateral sourcing deviation evaluated at a symmetric profile; Lemma~\ref{lem:global-concavity} makes that condition sufficient for the firm's constrained best response.

\begin{proposition}[Corrected symmetric Cournot action]
\label{prop:symmetric-cournot}
Under \eqref{eq:source-soc}, the unique symmetric pure Stage-I action is
\begin{equation}
i_C^*
=
\min\left\{
\phi,\,
\frac{HND}{b(N+1)^2-H^2N}
\right\}.
\label{eq:iCstar}
\end{equation}
Among admissible integer market sizes that satisfy the source restrictions at the compared values, \(i_C^*\) is weakly decreasing in \(N\), and is strictly decreasing when both compared actions are off the sourcing cap.
\end{proposition}

For the continuous extension of the interior root, holding the source primitives
\(a,H,\gamma,\phi,b\) fixed (and hence holding \(D\) fixed),
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\bar i_C}{\partial N}
=
-
\frac{HDb(N^2-1)}
{\left[b(N+1)^2-H^2N\right]^2}
<0
\qquad(N>1).
\label{eq:dIdN}
\end{equation}
Equation \eqref{eq:dIdN} reverses the sign reported in equation (14) and Proposition 3 of \cite{ShyStenbacka2005}.  The derivative is used only as a sign calculation; the economic comparison is across admissible integer values of \(N\).  This is uniqueness of the symmetric pure action, not uniqueness of the full general-\(N\) Stage-I equilibrium set.

The cap also matters for Corollary 4.  When \(\phi\) changes, so does
\(D=a-H\phi-\gamma\phi^2/2\).  On the cap branch \(i_C^*=\phi\), the outsourced number rises one-for-one with \(\phi\).  On the interior branch, \(\bar i_C\) falls with \(\phi\), and the outsourced fraction \(i_C^*/\phi\) is weakly decreasing after the cap and interior branches are combined.  Thus outsourced numbers and outsourced fractions must be distinguished.

\subsection{Source-duopoly global best response}

For \(N=2\), normalize
\begin{equation}
\delta=\frac{D}{H}>0,
\qquad
\rho=\frac{b}{H^2}>\frac49 .
\label{eq:normalized}
\end{equation}
Let \(y\) be rival sourcing and \(x\) own sourcing.  Define
\begin{equation}
U(y)=\delta+2y,\qquad
A(y)=\frac{2(\delta-y)}{9\rho-4},\qquad
M=\frac{\delta}{4\rho-1}.
\label{eq:BR-components}
\end{equation}
The first expression is the sourcing level at which the rival is just driven to zero downstream output; \(A(y)\) is the both-active stationary point; and \(M\) is the monopoly-branch stationary point.

For \(y\ge\delta\), let \(R(y)=0\).  For \(0\le y<\delta\), the unconstrained global response is
\begin{equation}
R(y)=
\begin{cases}
A(y),
& \rho\ge 2/3,\\[3pt]
U(y),
& 1/2\le\rho<2/3,\quad 0\le y\le y_A,\\
A(y),
& 1/2\le\rho<2/3,\quad y_A\le y<\delta,\\[3pt]
M,
& 4/9<\rho<1/2,\quad 0\le y\le y_M,\\
U(y),
& 4/9<\rho<1/2,\quad y_M\le y\le y_A,\\
A(y),
& 4/9<\rho<1/2,\quad y_A\le y<\delta,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:R}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
y_A=
\frac{\delta(2-3\rho)}{2(3\rho-1)},
\qquad
y_M=
\frac{\delta(1-2\rho)}{4\rho-1}.
\label{eq:yA-yM}
\end{equation}
The \(y_M\) branch is relevant only when \(\rho<1/2\).  The source-domain best response is
\begin{equation}
B_\phi(y)=\min\{\phi,R(y)\}.
\label{eq:Bphi}
\end{equation}

For \(4/9<\rho<2/3\), define
\[
\ell=
\begin{cases}
0,&1/2\le\rho<2/3,\\
y_M,&4/9<\rho<1/2,
\end{cases}
\qquad
J_\phi=
\left\{
y\in[0,\phi]:
\ell\le y\le y_A,\;
\delta+2y\le\phi
\right\}.
\]
On \(J_\phi\), the source-domain response equals the rival-exit response,
\[
B_\phi(y)=U(y)=\delta+2y.
\]
The set \(J_\phi\) has positive length if and only if
\begin{equation}
\begin{cases}
\phi>\delta,&1/2\le\rho<2/3,\\[2pt]
\phi>M=\delta/(4\rho-1),&4/9<\rho<1/2.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:exit-segment-feasibility}
\end{equation}
At equality the feasible exit piece degenerates to a single point.

\begin{proposition}[Global failure of strategic substitutability]
\label{prop:prop5}
For \(4/9<\rho<2/3\), the unconstrained pure global response \(R\) contains the rival-exit piece
\[
R(y)=U(y)=\delta+2y
\]
with slope \(+2\).  After imposing the source cap, a positive-length increasing piece survives in \(B_\phi\) exactly under \eqref{eq:exit-segment-feasibility}.  Consequently the source model admits parameter values for which the pure global sourcing best response is not decreasing, so Proposition 5 of \cite{ShyStenbacka2005} is false as an unqualified global strategic-substitutes claim.
\end{proposition}

The feasibility condition matters.  For example, at
\((\rho,\delta,\phi)=(3/5,1,1/10)\), the unconstrained response starts with
\(R(y)=1+2y\), but \(R(y)>\phi\) throughout the feasible rival-action
interval and hence \(B_\phi(y)\equiv\phi\).  This small-cap case does not
restore the published global theorem; it shows why the counterexample claim must
be existential rather than universal in the cap.  Where the exit piece survives,
its positive slope is generated by the participation threshold: a firm choosing
to induce rival exit must raise its own sourcing as rival sourcing increases.
This does not imply that outsourcing is globally a strategic complement, because
other pieces of the same response are decreasing or flat.

Figure~\ref{fig:br-witness} shows the exact regression \((\rho,\delta,\phi)=(3/5,1,2)\).  In that case
\[
B_\phi(y)=
\begin{cases}
1+2y,&0\le y\le1/8,\\
\frac{10}{7}(1-y),&1/8\le y<1,\\
0,&1\le y\le2,
\end{cases}
\]
and the best-response graphs intersect at three pure equilibria.
