\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Product-market competition can affect not only firms' prices and quantities but also their organizational boundaries.  Existing theory and evidence do not imply a uniformly signed relationship between competition and vertical integration or organizational disintegration.  In theory, \cite{DeBettignies2006} shows that stronger product-market competition can favor vertical disintegration, while \cite{AghionGriffithHowitt2006} derives a non-monotone relationship between competition and vertical integration.  Empirically, \cite{McGowan2017} finds that an exogenous increase in product-market competition reduced vertical integration in U.S. coal mining, whereas \cite{StiebaleVencappa2022} finds that import competition increased backward vertical integration among Indian manufacturing firms.  These studies concern organizational and ownership margins that are distinct from component outsourcing, so they are not direct tests of the model studied here.  Their contrasting results nevertheless make it important to characterize correctly the comparative-static implication of a given benchmark model.

Against this background, Shy and Stenbacka \cite{ShyStenbacka2005} study partial outsourcing when outsourcing lowers a firm's marginal production cost but creates monitoring costs.  Their two product-market specifications---homogeneous-good Cournot competition and differentiated Hotelling price competition---lead to sharp claims about how market structure changes outsourcing incentives.  In particular, they report that intensified Cournot competition enlarges the equilibrium set of outsourced components relative to those produced in-house.  This paper revisits those claims using the strategy bounds and product-market corner regimes already implied by the published model.

The correction has two layers.  The main layer concerns Cournot competition.  First, the derivative reported in equation (14) has the wrong sign.  Once the published symmetric interior expression is differentiated correctly, outsourcing per firm falls rather than rises with the number of Cournot competitors.  After restoring the primitive sourcing bound \(0\le i_j\le\phi\), the corrected symmetric pure Stage-I action is
\[
i_C^*
=
\min\left\{
\phi,\,
\frac{HND}{b(N+1)^2-H^2N}
\right\},
\]
where \(D\equiv a-H\phi-\gamma\phi^2/2>0\), and is weakly decreasing across admissible integer market sizes, strictly so when the compared actions are interior.

Second, the all-active Cournot continuation cannot be used after every feasible sourcing history.  A sufficiently asymmetric sourcing deviation can drive a downstream rival to zero output.  Solving the nonnegative-quantity continuation globally leaves each firm's reduced payoff strictly concave in its own sourcing choice under the source second-order condition, but it changes the global best-response geometry.  In the source duopoly, the unconstrained pure response contains the rival-exit piece
\[
i_j=\delta+2i_k,
\]
with slope \(+2\); after imposing the sourcing cap, a positive-length part of that piece survives only when the cap is sufficiently large.  Such admissible parameter values exist, so the regular all-active strategic-substitutes calculation behind Proposition 5 is local rather than a global theorem.

Third, that global response yields a complete pure Stage-I equilibrium correspondence for the source duopoly.  Depending on \(\rho=b/H^2\) and the sourcing cap, the game has a unique symmetric equilibrium, a knife-edge continuum, or three pure equilibria consisting of one symmetric equilibrium and an asymmetric pair.  These statements are deliberately confined to pure Stage-I strategies in the source duopoly; we do not claim a complete mixed-strategy correspondence or uniqueness of the general-\(N\) Stage-I game.

The Hotelling specification has a separate continuation problem.  With the literal source price strategy space, sufficiently asymmetric marginal costs produce a continuum of pure corner price equilibria.  The high-cost firm has zero demand and is indifferent over a range of prices, but those prices change the low-cost firm's equilibrium profit.  Hence the published interior backward induction does not define a single Stage-I continuation payoff at every feasible sourcing history.  As a robustness exercise, we also impose the explicit auxiliary restriction \(p_j\ge c_j\).  In that restricted pure-price game the continuation is unique, and the published symmetric sourcing candidate fails on an exact nonempty, cap-aware parameter region.  We do not interpret this restriction as elimination of all weakly dominated strategies: although every price below marginal cost is weakly dominated by marginal-cost pricing, marginal-cost pricing is itself weakly dominated by any fixed slightly higher price.

The contribution is therefore a source-specific correction and global re-characterization of a particular published model, not a new general theory of outsourcing and not an empirical resolution of the broader competition--firm-boundary relationship.  The corrected Cournot result reclassifies the comparative-static implication of the Shy--Stenbacka model within a literature in which the sign is mechanism- and margin-dependent: in the published model itself, the symmetric pure outsourcing action is weakly decreasing, rather than increasing, across admissible market sizes.  A negative competition effect on outsourcing also arises in the marginal-cost-saving variant studied by \cite{Konig2010}, but that related result does not establish a generic outsourcing theorem.  Nor do we claim generic novelty for asymmetry or multiplicity, which are established possibilities in the strategic-investment literature \cite{Amir2000,AmirGarciaKnauff2010,AmirHalmenschlagerJin2011,EckertKlumppSu2017}.  Our purpose is not to resolve the mixed empirical relationship between competition and firm boundaries, but to establish the correct implications of the Shy--Stenbacka model itself once its published strategy bounds and downstream corner regimes are enforced.

The paper proceeds as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:model} states the source primitives and exact strategy domains.  Section~\ref{sec:bestresponses} solves the global Cournot continuation, derives the corrected symmetric comparative static, and constructs the source-duopoly global best response.  Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium} gives the complete pure Stage-I duopoly correspondence.  Section~\ref{sec:hotelling} analyzes the literal Hotelling continuation and the auxiliary no-loss robustness game.  Section~\ref{sec:welfare-scope} records welfare and robustness limits, and Section~\ref{sec:literature} positions the correction relative to the surrounding literature.  Technical proofs are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
