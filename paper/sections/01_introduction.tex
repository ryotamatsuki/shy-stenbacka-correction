\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Shy and Stenbacka \cite{ShyStenbacka2005} study partial outsourcing when outsourcing lowers a firm's marginal production cost but creates monitoring costs.  Their two product-market specifications---homogeneous-good Cournot competition and differentiated Hotelling price competition---lead to sharp claims about how market structure changes outsourcing incentives.  This paper revisits those claims using the strategy bounds and product-market corner regimes already implied by the published model.

The correction has two layers.  The main layer concerns Cournot competition.  First, the derivative reported in equation (14) has the wrong sign.  Once the published symmetric interior expression is differentiated correctly, outsourcing per firm falls rather than rises with the number of Cournot competitors.  After restoring the primitive sourcing bound \(0\le i_j\le\phi\), the corrected symmetric pure Stage-I action is
\[
i_C^*
=
\min\left\{
\phi,\,
\frac{HND}{b(N+1)^2-H^2N}
\right\},
\]
and is weakly decreasing across admissible integer market sizes, strictly so when the compared actions are interior.

Second, the all-active Cournot continuation cannot be used after every feasible sourcing history.  A sufficiently asymmetric sourcing deviation can drive a downstream rival to zero output.  Solving the nonnegative-quantity continuation globally leaves each firm's reduced payoff strictly concave in its own sourcing choice under the source second-order condition, but it changes the global best-response geometry.  In the source duopoly, the pure global best response can contain the rival-exit segment
\[
i_j=\delta+2i_k,
\]
which has slope \(+2\).  Thus the regular all-active strategic-substitutes calculation behind Proposition 5 is local rather than global.

Third, that global response yields a complete pure Stage-I equilibrium correspondence for the source duopoly.  Depending on \(\rho=b/H^2\) and the sourcing cap, the game has a unique symmetric equilibrium, a knife-edge continuum, or three pure equilibria consisting of one symmetric equilibrium and an asymmetric pair.  These statements are deliberately confined to pure Stage-I strategies in the source duopoly; we do not claim a complete mixed-strategy correspondence or uniqueness of the general-\(N\) Stage-I game.

The Hotelling specification has a separate continuation problem.  With the literal source price strategy space, sufficiently asymmetric marginal costs produce a continuum of pure corner price equilibria.  The high-cost firm has zero demand and is indifferent over a range of prices, but those prices change the low-cost firm's equilibrium profit.  Hence the published interior backward induction does not define a single Stage-I continuation payoff at every feasible sourcing history.  As a robustness exercise, we also impose the explicit auxiliary restriction \(p_j\ge c_j\).  In that restricted pure-price game the continuation is unique, and the published symmetric sourcing candidate fails on an exact nonempty, cap-aware parameter region.  We do not interpret this restriction as elimination of all weakly dominated strategies: although every price below marginal cost is weakly dominated by marginal-cost pricing, marginal-cost pricing is itself weakly dominated by any fixed slightly higher price.

The contribution is therefore a correction and global re-characterization of a particular published model, not a new general theory of outsourcing or investment games.  Negative competition effects on outsourcing arise in related specifications \cite{Konig2010}, while asymmetric investment, endogenous exit, and multiple equilibria are well established in the strategic-investment literature \cite{Amir2000,AmirGarciaKnauff2010,AmirHalmenschlagerJin2011,EckertKlumppSu2017}.  What is specific here is that these global features appear inside the published Shy--Stenbacka source model once the primitive sourcing constraint and downstream active-set changes are respected.

The paper proceeds as follows.  Section~\ref{sec:model} states the source primitives and exact strategy domains.  Section~\ref{sec:bestresponses} solves the global Cournot continuation, derives the corrected symmetric comparative static, and constructs the source-duopoly global best response.  Section~\ref{sec:equilibrium} gives the complete pure Stage-I duopoly correspondence.  Section~\ref{sec:hotelling} analyzes the literal Hotelling continuation and the auxiliary no-loss robustness game.  Section~\ref{sec:welfare-scope} records welfare and robustness limits, and Section~\ref{sec:literature} positions the correction relative to the surrounding literature.  Technical proofs are collected in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.
