\section{Pure equilibrium correspondence}
\label{sec:equilibrium}

The global response \eqref{eq:Bphi} changes the equilibrium set qualitatively.  Define the symmetric fixed point
\begin{equation}
s=\frac{2\delta}{9\rho-2}.
\label{eq:s}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Complete pure Stage-I correspondence for the source duopoly]
\label{prop:correspondence}
Under the source assumptions and \(\rho>4/9\), the complete set of pure Stage-I equilibria is as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
\item If \(\rho>2/3\), the unique pure equilibrium is
\[
(\min\{\phi,s\},\,\min\{\phi,s\}).
\]
\item If \(\rho=2/3\) and \(\phi<\delta/2\), the unique pure equilibrium is \((\phi,\phi)\).
\item If \(\rho=2/3\) and \(\phi\ge\delta/2\), the complete pure equilibrium set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal E=
\left\{
(x,\delta-x):
x\in[\max\{0,\delta-\phi\},\,\min\{\phi,\delta\}]
\right\}.
\label{eq:knife-continuum}
\end{equation}
\item If \(4/9<\rho<2/3\) and \(\phi\le s\), the unique pure equilibrium is \((\phi,\phi)\).
\item If \(4/9<\rho<2/3\) and \(\phi>s\), there are exactly three pure equilibria:
\begin{equation}
(s,s),\qquad(x_H,x_L),\qquad(x_L,x_H),
\label{eq:three-eq}
\end{equation}
where
\[
x_H=\min\{\phi,R(0)\},
\qquad
x_L=R(x_H).
\]
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

For the exact regression used in Figure~\ref{fig:br-witness},
\begin{equation}
(\rho,\delta,\phi)=\left(\frac35,1,2\right),
\label{eq:regression}
\end{equation}
the three equilibria are
\begin{equation}
\left(\frac{10}{17},\frac{10}{17}\right),
\qquad
(1,0),
\qquad
(0,1).
\label{eq:regression-eq}
\end{equation}
The coexistence of a symmetric equilibrium and an asymmetric pair occurs even though each player's reduced payoff is globally strictly concave in its own sourcing action.  The source of multiplicity is therefore not own-payoff nonconcavity; it is the change in downstream participation that alters the global best-response geometry.

For \(\rho>2/3\), the active branch has slope magnitude
\[
\left|\frac{-2}{9\rho-4}\right|<1,
\]
and the capped/zero pieces have slope zero, so the two-player best-response operator is a contraction.  At \(\rho=2/3\), the response becomes \(B_\phi(y)=\min\{\phi,\max\{0,\delta-y\}\}\), which yields the exact continuum \eqref{eq:knife-continuum}.  For \(4/9<\rho<2/3\), a branch-by-branch intersection of the complete response graph with its transpose yields exactly the three fixed points in \eqref{eq:three-eq} whenever the cap lies above the symmetric action.
