\subsection{Proof of Lemmas \ref{lem:cournot-continuation} and \ref{lem:global-concavity}}

For fixed marginal costs, the first derivative of firm \(j\)'s quantity payoff is
\[
\frac{\partial\pi_j}{\partial q_j}
=
a-c_j-bQ-bq_j.
\]
The potential \eqref{eq:potential} has the same gradient and Hessian
\[
\nabla^2\Phi=-b(I+\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^\top).
\]
For every nonzero vector \(v\),
\[
v^\top\nabla^2\Phi v
=
-b\left(\lVert v\rVert^2+(\mathbf 1^\top v)^2\right)<0,
\]
so \(\Phi\) is strictly concave.  Existence also requires control of the
noncompact feasible set.  If
\(L=\max_j|a-c_j|\), then on \(\mathbb R_+^N\)
\[
\Phi(q)
\le
L\sqrt N\,\lVert q\rVert
-\frac b2\lVert q\rVert^2,
\]
and hence \(\Phi(q)\to-\infty\) as \(\lVert q\rVert\to\infty\).
Thus a maximizer exists on a compact upper level set and is unique by strict
concavity.

The potential KKT conditions coincide with the firms' quantity best-response
conditions.  If \(q_j>0\), they imply
\[
p-c_j=bq_j>0,
\]
whereas if \(q_j=0\), they imply \(p-c_j\le0\).  For an active set
\(S\), summing \(q_j=(p-c_j)/b\) over \(j\in S\) and using
\(p=a-bQ\) gives
\[
p=p_S=\frac{a+\sum_{j\in S}c_j}{|S|+1}.
\]
Consequently \(c_j<p_S\) for active firms and \(c_j\ge p_S\) for inactive
firms, with equality assigned to the zero-output side.  Equal marginal costs do
not create another quantity vector because the potential maximizer is unique.
This proves Lemma~\ref{lem:cournot-continuation}.

Now fix rivals' sourcing and increase \(x=i_j\).  If firm \(j\) is inactive,
its reduced payoff is \(-x^2\).  Once it is active together with \(m-1\)
other firms, write \(z_j=p-c_j>0\).  On that smooth branch,
\[
\frac{dp}{dx}=-\frac{H}{m+1},
\qquad
\frac{dz_j}{dx}=H\frac{m}{m+1},
\]
and operating profit is \(z_j^2/b\).  Hence
\[
\Pi_j''(x)
=
2\frac{H^2m^2}{b(m+1)^2}-2<0
\]
under \eqref{eq:source-soc}, because \(m/(m+1)\le N/(N+1)\).

These branch calculations can be joined globally.  While \(j\) is inactive,
changing \(x\) does not affect the downstream equilibrium.  At its entry
threshold \(z_j=0\), the derivative of operating profit is zero, so the
one-sided derivatives of the reduced payoff both equal \(-2x\).  After entry,
\(z_j\) increases strictly with \(x\), so firm \(j\) cannot exit again.
At the same time the equilibrium price is weakly decreasing in \(x\).
Because rivals' marginal costs are fixed, a rival may therefore exit but cannot
re-enter.  With finitely many firms there are only finitely many such switches.

If \(r\ge1\) rivals leave simultaneously when the total active count falls
from \(m\) to \(m-r\), continuity of the Cournot quantity vector gives
continuity of the reduced payoff, while the operating-profit part of its
one-sided derivative falls by
\[
\frac{2Hz_j}{b}
\left[
\frac{m}{m+1}
-
\frac{m-r}{m-r+1}
\right]
>0.
\]
(The case \(r=1\) is the jump displayed in the main text.)  Thus the
one-sided derivative is strictly decreasing on every smooth piece, joins
continuously at own entry, and jumps only downward at rival exits.  This
monotonicity of one-sided derivatives, rather than branchwise concavity alone,
establishes global strict concavity and proves
Lemma~\ref{lem:global-concavity}.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:symmetric-cournot}}

At a symmetric profile \(s\), each firm produces
\[
q(s)=\frac{D+Hs}{b(N+1)}.
\]
The derivative of reduced payoff with respect to a unilateral sourcing choice,
evaluated at symmetry, is
\[
\frac{2HN(D+Hs)}{b(N+1)^2}-2s.
\]
Its unconstrained zero is \eqref{eq:iCbar}.  The source condition
\[
b>\left(\frac{HN}{N+1}\right)^2
\]
implies
\[
b(N+1)^2-H^2N
>
H^2N(N-1)>0
\qquad(N\ge2),
\]
so the root is well defined and positive.  Lemma~\ref{lem:global-concavity}
makes the unilateral first-order/KKT condition globally sufficient, while
\(D>0\) rules out the lower bound.  Projection onto \([0,\phi]\) gives
\eqref{eq:iCstar}.  This proves uniqueness of the symmetric pure action only;
it does not establish uniqueness of all general-\(N\) Stage-I equilibria.

For the competition comparison, hold \(a,H,\gamma,\phi,b\) fixed, so
\(D=a-H\phi-\gamma\phi^2/2\) is fixed, and differentiate the continuous
extension to obtain \eqref{eq:dIdN}.  The economic comparison then uses only
admissible integer values of \(N\).  When \(\phi\) rather than \(N\)
changes, \(D\) changes with it:
\[
\frac{dD}{d\phi}=-H-\gamma\phi<0.
\]
Consequently the interior sourcing level falls with \(\phi\), whereas the
cap branch has \(i_C^*=\phi\).  The number outsourced and the outsourced
fraction therefore have to be treated separately across the cap switch.

\subsection{Derivation of the source-duopoly response and Proposition \ref{prop:prop5}}

Normalize as in \eqref{eq:normalized}.  Against rival sourcing \(y\), the
reduced payoff has the relevant pieces
\[
-x^2,
\qquad
\frac{(\delta+2x-y)^2}{9\rho}-x^2,
\qquad
\frac{(\delta+x)^2}{4\rho}-x^2,
\]
for own inactivity, both-active output, and rival inactivity, respectively.
The both-active stationary point, the rival-exit boundary, and the monopoly
stationary point are therefore
\[
A(y)=\frac{2(\delta-y)}{9\rho-4},
\qquad
U(y)=\delta+2y,
\qquad
M=\frac{\delta}{4\rho-1}.
\]
The relevant joins solve
\[
A(y_A)=U(y_A),
\qquad
M=U(y_M),
\]
and give \eqref{eq:yA-yM}.  Moreover
\[
y_A-y_M
=
\frac{\delta\rho}
{2(3\rho-1)(4\rho-1)}
>0
\]
whenever \(4/9<\rho<1/2\).  At \(\rho=1/2\),
\[
y_M=0,\qquad M=\delta,\qquad y_A=\delta/2,
\]
so the two neighboring descriptions join continuously.  The one-sided
derivative signs together with Lemma~\ref{lem:global-concavity} give the
unconstrained branch sequence in \eqref{eq:R}; the source-domain response is
then \(B_\phi(y)=\min\{\phi,R(y)\}\).

For \(1/2\le\rho<2/3\), the exit piece of \(R\) is
\(U(y)\) on \([0,y_A]\).  Its feasible part after the cap is
\[
0\le y\le
\min\left\{y_A,\frac{\phi-\delta}{2}\right\}.
\]
It has positive length exactly when \(\phi>\delta\); at
\(\phi=\delta\) it degenerates to \(y=0\).  For
\(4/9<\rho<1/2\), the exit piece begins at \(y_M\), and
\[
U(y_M)=M.
\]
Because \(M>y_M\), a positive-length feasible piece survives exactly when
\(\phi>M\); at \(\phi=M\) only the join point remains.  These are exactly
the conditions in \eqref{eq:exit-segment-feasibility}.  On the interior of
the surviving set,
\[
B_\phi'(y)=U'(y)=2.
\]
This proves Proposition~\ref{prop:prop5}, including its cap qualification.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:correspondence}}

A pure Stage-I equilibrium solves
\[
x=B_\phi(y),\qquad y=B_\phi(x).
\]

\paragraph{Case \(\rho>2/3\).}
Here the uncapped response is the active branch \(A\) for
\(0\le y<\delta\) and zero for \(y\ge\delta\).  It is continuous at
\(\delta\).  The active slope has magnitude
\[
k=\frac{2}{9\rho-4}<1.
\]
Applying the cap adds only flat pieces and preserves continuity.  Hence the
continuous piecewise-affine map \(B_\phi\) is globally Lipschitz with
constant \(k<1\).  Therefore
\[
T(x,y)=(B_\phi(y),B_\phi(x))
\]
maps the complete square \([0,\phi]^2\) into itself and is a contraction in
the sup norm.  It has a unique fixed point.  Since \(T\) commutes with the
coordinate swap, uniqueness forces that fixed point to be symmetric; solving
the symmetric condition gives
\[
(\min\{\phi,s\},\min\{\phi,s\}).
\]

\paragraph{Case \(\rho=2/3\).}
The exact response is
\[
B_\phi(y)=\min\{\phi,\max\{0,\delta-y\}\}.
\]
If \(\phi<\delta/2\), then \(\delta-y>\phi\) for every feasible
\(y\), so \(B_\phi(y)=\phi\) throughout the strategy interval and the
only equilibrium is \((\phi,\phi)\).  If \(\phi\ge\delta/2\), mutual
best response is equivalent to
\[
x+y=\delta,\qquad 0\le x,y\le\phi,
\]
which gives \eqref{eq:knife-continuum}.  At
\(\phi=\delta/2\) the interval collapses to the single point
\((\delta/2,\delta/2)\); it is nondegenerate only for
\(\phi>\delta/2\).  When \(\phi\ge\delta\), its endpoints are
\((0,\delta)\) and \((\delta,0)\).

\paragraph{Case \(4/9<\rho<2/3\).}
First note
\[
y_A<s<\delta,
\]
because
\[
s-y_A
=
\frac{3\delta\rho(9\rho-4)}
{2(3\rho-1)(9\rho-2)}
>0,
\qquad
\delta-s
=
\frac{\delta(9\rho-4)}{9\rho-2}
>0.
\]
On the active branch,
\[
A(z)-z
=
\frac{2\delta-(9\rho-2)z}{9\rho-4}.
\]
On the exit branch, \(U(z)-z=\delta+z>0\), and on the monopoly branch
\(M-z>0\) throughout its domain because \(z\le y_M<M\).  Together with
\(R(z)=0\) for \(z\ge\delta\), these observations give the crossing
property
\[
R(z)>z\ (z<s),\qquad
R(s)=s,\qquad
R(z)<z\ (z>s).
\]
The same strict inequalities hold for \(B_\phi\) away from a binding cap.

If \(\phi\le s\), then
\[
B_\phi(z)>z\quad(0\le z<\phi),
\qquad
B_\phi(\phi)=\phi.
\]
If a mutual best response had \(x<y\), then either
\(y<\phi\), in which case \(x=B_\phi(y)>y\), or
\(y=\phi\), in which case \(x=B_\phi(y)=\phi\).  Both are
contradictions.  Thus the unique equilibrium is \((\phi,\phi)\), including
the boundary \(\phi=s\).

Now suppose \(\phi>s\).  The symmetric equilibrium is \((s,s)\).  For an
asymmetric equilibrium label the actions \(x_H>x_L\).  The crossing property
implies
\[
x_L<s<x_H.
\]
There are two exhaustive cases.

If \(x_H<\delta\), then \(x_H>s>y_A\), so the low action is
\[
x_L=B_\phi(x_H)=A(x_H)<s<\phi;
\]
the cap cannot bind for the low action.  Consider the high action
\(x_H=B_\phi(x_L)\).  If its cap did not bind and \(x_L\) lay on the
active branch, then
\[
x_H=A(x_L),\qquad x_L=A(x_H).
\]
Since \(A\) is affine with slope \(-2/(9\rho-4)\ne-1\), subtraction of
these two equations forces \(x_H=x_L\), contrary to asymmetry.  If the high
action instead came from the uncapped exit branch, then
\(x_H=U(x_L)=\delta+2x_L\ge\delta\), again a contradiction.  If
\(\rho<1/2\) and it came from the uncapped monopoly branch, then
\[
x_H=M>\delta,
\]
because \(4\rho-1<1\).  The zero branch cannot generate the high action.
Therefore the high action must be capped:
\[
x_H=\phi<\delta,
\qquad
x_L=A(\phi).
\]
This pair is indeed mutual.  If \(x_L\) lies on the exit or monopoly branch,
then \(R(x_L)\ge\delta>\phi\).  If it lies on the active branch, direct
substitution gives
\[
A(A(\phi))-\phi
=
\frac{
3(3\rho-2)\,[2\delta-(9\rho-2)\phi]
}{(9\rho-4)^2}
>0,
\]
because \(\rho<2/3\) and \(\phi>s\).  Hence in every subcase
\(B_\phi(x_L)=\phi=x_H\).

If \(x_H\ge\delta\), then by definition of the global response
\[
x_L=B_\phi(x_H)=0.
\]
Mutual best response then requires
\[
x_H=B_\phi(0)=\min\{\phi,R(0)\}.
\]
Conversely this candidate is feasible whenever it belongs to the present case:
for \(1/2\le\rho<2/3\), \(R(0)=\delta\); for
\(4/9<\rho<1/2\), \(R(0)=M>\delta\).  Thus
\(\phi\ge\delta\) makes \(x_H\ge\delta\), including equality, and
\(R(x_H)=0\).

The two cases fit the common formula
\[
x_H=\min\{\phi,R(0)\},
\qquad
x_L=R(x_H).
\]
In the first case \(x_H<\delta\) and \(x_L=A(x_H)<\phi\), so no
additional cap is needed on \(x_L\); in the second case \(x_L=0\).
The ordered asymmetric pair is therefore unique, and its mirror is the only
other asymmetric equilibrium.  Together with \((s,s)\), this proves the
exactly-three statement without relying on numerical enumeration.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:literal-hotelling}}

Fix a rival price \(r\) and own marginal cost \(c\).  Under the maintained
full-coverage clipped share, direct maximization over the literal price domain
gives
\[
BR_c(r)=
\begin{cases}
[r+\tau,\infty),
& r\le c-\tau,\\[3pt]
\left\{\dfrac{r+\tau+c}{2}\right\},
& c-\tau<r<c+3\tau,\\[8pt]
\{r-\tau\},
& r\ge c+3\tau.
\end{cases}
\]
At \(r=c-\tau\), every price at or above \(r+\tau=c\) yields zero
demand and zero profit, while a positive-demand price would be below cost.  At
\(r=c+3\tau\), the interior vertex and the full-market boundary coincide.
If prices are restricted to \(\mathbb R_+\) rather than \(\mathbb R\),
the first set is intersected with \(\mathbb R_+\); the equilibrium set below
is unchanged because source costs and all equilibrium prices are positive.

Solving the two best-response conditions gives the familiar interior prices
when \(|d|<3\tau\).  At \(|d|=3\tau\), the interior solution reaches the
full-market/zero-demand boundary and the equilibrium is a single point.  If
\(d=c_B-c_A>3\tau\), let \(p_B=z\).  Firm A's full-market best response
requires
\[
p_A=z-\tau,
\qquad
z\ge c_A+3\tau.
\]
Given this \(p_A\), firm B optimally accepts zero demand precisely when
\[
z\le c_B.
\]
Hence the mutual-best-response set is exactly
\[
p_B=z,qquad p_A=z-\tau,
\qquad
z\in[c_A+3\tau,c_B].
\]
Values below the lower endpoint put A on its interior branch; values above the
upper endpoint give B a profitable positive-demand response, so there are no
additional corner combinations.  The case \(d<-3\tau\) is symmetric.
Throughout the corner interval B earns zero, while A's operating profit is
\(n(z-\tau-c_A)\), which varies with \(z\).  This proves
Proposition~\ref{prop:literal-hotelling}.

\subsection{Weak-dominance facts}

Let own marginal cost be \(c\).  For every rival price \(r\) and every
\(p<c\),
\[
(p-c)s(p,r)\le0,
\]
whereas \(p=c\) yields exactly zero operating profit.  There are admissible
rival prices for which the below-cost action has positive demand and therefore
strictly negative profit, so \(p=c\) weakly dominates each fixed \(p<c\).
For every fixed \(\varepsilon>0\),
\[
(c+\varepsilon-c)s(c+\varepsilon,r)
=
\varepsilon s(c+\varepsilon,r)\ge0
\]
for all \(r\), while \(p=c\) still yields zero.  Choosing, for example, a
rival price high enough to give \(c+\varepsilon\) positive demand makes the
inequality strict.  Thus \(c+\varepsilon\) weakly dominates \(c\) as
well.  The comparison uses the demand induced by each own price separately.
Therefore the auxiliary restriction \(p\ge c\) cannot be interpreted as
elimination of all weakly dominated prices.

\subsection{Proof of Proposition \ref{prop:no-loss}}

Under \eqref{eq:no-loss}, the literal corner family for \(d>3\tau\) can
satisfy the high-cost firm's no-loss constraint only at
\[
p_B=c_B,\qquad p_A=c_B-\tau.
\]
No additional restricted corner equilibrium is created: if \(p_B>c_B\) and A
sets the full-market boundary \(p_A=p_B-\tau\), then B is in its
positive-demand response region and can profitably deviate.  The mirror argument
applies for \(d<-3\tau\), so the restricted pure-price continuation is
unique.  Substitution gives \eqref{eq:no-loss-payoff}.

Against the symmetric interior candidate \(y=i_0=Hn/6\), the strict source
condition gives
\[
i_0=\frac{Hn}{6}<\frac{3\tau}{H},
\]
so the own-inactive branch would require a negative feasible action and cannot
be reached from \(x\ge0\).  On the interior branch,
\(V_{i_0}^{NL}(x)\) is strictly concave and has its stationary point at
\(x=i_0\); hence no interior deviation is profitable.  The only remaining
candidate for a strictly profitable nonlocal deviation is the low-cost
full-market branch, which starts at
\[
x_B=i_0+\frac{3\tau}{H}.
\]
Relative to the symmetric payoff its gain is
\begin{equation}
\Delta(x)
=
-\frac{
36x^2-36Hnx+5H^2n^2+54n\tau
}{36}.
\label{eq:delta-x}
\end{equation}
This is a concave quadratic with vertex
\[
x_M=\frac{Hn}{2},
\qquad
\Delta(x_M)
=
\frac{n(2H^2n-27\tau)}{18}.
\]

If \(H^2n<27\tau/2\), the vertex gain is negative; at equality it is zero.
Thus no strictly profitable deviation exists in either case.  Now suppose
\[
\frac{27}{2}\tau<H^2n<18\tau.
\]
The two roots are real, and the lower one is \(x_-\) in
\eqref{eq:xminus}.  Writing
\(\lambda=H^2n/\tau\in(27/2,18)\), direct comparison gives
\[
\frac{x_B}{Hn}
=
\frac16+\frac3\lambda
<
\frac12
-
\frac16\sqrt{\frac{2(2\lambda-27)}{\lambda}}
=
\frac{x_-}{Hn}
<
\frac{x_M}{Hn}
=
\frac12.
\]
The first inequality becomes equality only at \(\lambda=18\), which is
outside the maintained strict source condition.  Hence the positive-gain
interval begins strictly inside the correct full-market branch.  If
\(\phi\le x_-\), no feasible action has positive gain.  If
\(x_-<\phi\le x_M\), any action in \((x_-,\phi]\) sufficiently close
to \(\phi\) has positive gain; if \(\phi>x_M\), the vertex itself is
feasible and profitable.  Thus strict failure is equivalent to
\(\phi>x_-\), while \(\phi=x_-\) gives a tie.

The upper equality \(H^2n=18\tau\) is excluded by the source's strict
second-order condition.  Finally, substituting
\(H=n=1\), \(\tau=1/15\), and \(x=1/2\) into
\eqref{eq:delta-x} gives the exact gain \(1/90\).  With
\(H=n=1\), \(\tau=5/72\), the lower root is \(x_-=5/12\), so choosing
\(\phi=5/12\) gives the cap-equality tie exactly.
